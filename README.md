Hi, I'm Harish. I'm a software engineer at [Purgo AI](https://www.purgo.ai/) in the San Francisco Bay Area, working on agents, retrieval and evals ([write-ups](https://harishmanoharan.com/work)).

I studied biology and then data science at IIT Madras. After that came an M.S. in computer science at UT Dallas.

Things I've built on my own:

- **[ReadableSTEM](https://readablestem.com)** turns STEM course PDFs into accessible web pages, with equations as MathML a screen reader can speak. It's a free, open-source beta that anyone can sign in to. I evaluated it on a pre-registered held-out set of 15 OpenStax sections: heading F1 0.96 and 97% of figure descriptions passed by a model judge, while on whole-page equations a Claude Opus chatbot still scores higher, 0.62 to its 0.50. A second held-out set of 15 arXiv papers tests it on real LaTeX PDFs, where it scores 0.58 on equations to the chatbots' 0.64, too close for that set to separate ([write-up](https://harishmanoharan.com/work/readablestem/), [source](https://github.com/harishm17/readablestem)).
- **[Margins](https://studymargins.com)** turns your own course files into notes, worked examples, quizzes and timed practice exams, each citing the pages it came from, plus a voice coach. Give it your exam date and it plans each day's work and tracks what you've mastered from your quizzes and exams ([source](https://github.com/harishm17/margins)).
- **[Holdbrief](https://holdbrief.com)** is a daily brief on the stocks you follow, for US and Indian markets, free during its beta. Fixed scoring methods set each stock's stance, and Claude writes the explanation; every sentence has to cite the facts behind it, and a checker rejects any that don't.
